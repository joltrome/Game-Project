Evidence

What we know

VM-0.2.3-A was reported as much more engaging under normal play, but wall hugging was a dominant safe-zone exploit because no lane covered the extreme reachable player positions.

Hypothesis

Sustained edge targeting

A one-second edge dwell followed by a retained, normally warned edge-containing pattern may discourage stationary camping without punishing brief ordinary wall use.

Decision

Geometry first

Derive full lane coverage analytically from walls, player width, and can width; preserve all VM-0.2.3-A pacing and terrain values.

Result

Build VM-0.2.3-A-R1

Fourteen derived lanes cover the full 112–1040 player-center interval. Natural edge tests launched after 1.117 seconds on the left and 3.500 seconds on the right after threshold activation. Manual fairness remains unverified.

Prototype A

Compact arena

Agency-first

Free left/right movement and jumping inside a constrained vending-machine chamber.

Hypothesis

The arena creates intentional positioning, readable recovery, and stronger “I can do better” deaths.

Failure modes

  • Feels like a generic platform survival game.
  • Camping in one safe location dominates.
  • Jumping invalidates most hazards.
  • Too much empty movement between threats.

Machine-native justification

Products fall from visible coils or shelves; jams shake before releasing; refrigeration failures alter floor traction.

Prototype B

Conveyor scroll

Pressure-first

The environment automatically advances while the player has limited horizontal repositioning and jumping.

Hypothesis

Forced movement creates more immediate tension, rhythm, and shorter gaps between meaningful decisions.

Failure modes

  • Feels like a generic endless runner.
  • Forced motion combines with random falls to create unavoidable deaths.
  • The player watches patterns instead of making choices.
  • Limited repositioning makes controls feel unresponsive.

Machine-native justification

An internal delivery conveyor or product elevator drags the player toward the retrieval chute.

Playtest log

Control-feel gate

  • Left/right response is immediate without feeling binary.
  • Stopping distance is predictable.
  • Jump input is buffered shortly before landing.
  • Coyote time forgives a narrowly missed ledge.
  • Holding jump produces a deliberate, tested result.
  • Air control is useful but does not erase commitment.
  • Keyboard ghosting does not block movement + jump combinations.

Fairness gate

  • Every damaging object has a readable source and telegraph.
  • At least one reachable response exists when a hazard commits.
  • Spawn logic cannot seal all escape paths.
  • Player hitbox visually matches the sprite/shape.
  • Death cause is obvious without explanatory text.
  • Randomness changes situations, not whether survival is possible.
  • Difficulty increases through pressure, not hidden information.

Restart gate

  • Death feedback completes quickly.
  • Restart requires an intentional player input.
  • Restart returns directly to control, not a menu.
  • No forced tutorial repeats.
  • Run count and cause of death can be logged anonymously.
  • The tester is never verbally told to “try again.”

Visual-coherence gate

  • All gray-box objects use one consistent visual grammar.
  • Danger, background, collision, and decoration read differently.
  • Hazards originate from vending-machine mechanisms.
  • Camera framing shows needed reaction space.
  • No placeholder asset implies a mechanic that does not exist.
  • The conveyor has a physically legible source of motion.

Stop conditions

Generic

Testers describe it only as “Dodge the blocks” and cannot recall a vending-machine-specific event.

Unfair

Players repeatedly misidentify death causes or correctly report that no reachable response existed.

No pull

Players stop after one or two runs and need prompting to continue.
